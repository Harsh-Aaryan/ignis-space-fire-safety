# IGNIS fire model: verification, calibration and scientific limits

## Current model: 3.0.1 (2026-11-15)

This revision fixes experimental inputs and temperature postprocessing. Its results are **mixed**, not an across-the-board accuracy improvement. Median CO, CO₂ and outlet-temperature errors decrease. The worst held-out HRR, total-heat and gas errors increase after reconstructing the VI-3 airflow. The calibrated PMMA growth rate also transfers worse to Saffire II. These failures remain in the evaluation and interface.

### Corrections and their evidence

- **Fan history.** [NASA's VI presentation](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20240007488), slides 16–18, gives ignition at 637 s, reduction from 20 to 5 cm/s at 1120 s, and stop at 1420 s. Commands are therefore 483 s and 783 s after ignition. The last command is outside the table's rounded 780 s burn window. The eight-second fan response remains an assumption, not a measured flight response. Emergency fan-off takes precedence over scheduled commands.
- **Identical observation windows.** Both the benchmark and the CFAST history exporter stop at ignition plus the published duration, including a fractional final step. Previously the benchmark could overshoot a step and the exporter measured its horizon from heater start.
- **Corrected reproduction conditions.** [NASA's I–III test matrix](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20180005168), slides 23 and 30–31, gives approximately 10 cm of remaining fuel for Saffire-I I-2 and 25 cm/s flow for Saffire-II II-6. The original PSI CSVs and their transcription are preserved; explicit, sourced reproduction overrides carry these corrections. The SIBAL burnout-flux derivation now normalizes each strip for its own airflow and oxygen.
- **Outlet temperature.** Mix the modeled source stream with bypass cabin air using molar flow. The source temperature already includes fuel feedback and modeled wall losses; the old heat-release/duct-capacity estimate ignored those losses. Molar weighting accounts for hot-gas expansion and conserves stream enthalpy under the model's common heat-capacity assumption. A zero-flow duct has no defined mixed-mean outlet reading. No temperature coefficient was fitted.
- **Accuracy cues.** A constant-flow run no longer inherits VI-3's reported error: its fan history must match. Runs near VI-4 also remain extrapolated because its complete airflow history is unavailable. The model-check text distinguishes that limitation and both hot and cold outlet errors.

VI-4 also reduced flow, according to the [ICES paper](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20240002981), but the retrieved report does not give its command times. Its reproduction remains constant-flow and incomplete. No timing was invented to lower its error.

The later [ICES-2026-577 paper, uploaded by its author](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/410245330_Heat_and_Smoke_Emission_from_Tests_on_the_SAFFIRE_IV-VI_Experiments), Table 1, adds **900 s of initial flow for VI-4**, 60/120 s two-sided/one-sided igniter durations and measured flame-growth rates. It does not fully specify VI-4's subsequent fan commands. It also revises peak HRR, gas summaries and temperature-rise processing. These are a reanalysis of the same flights, not new independent validation cases. This revision keeps the 2024 benchmark edition intact rather than silently combining summary values from different analyses. The 2026 paper identifies back-side heat loss and narrow-strip edge loss as plausible reasons for different PMMA growth, consistent with the current model's transfer failure. The [2025 vehicle-modeling paper](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20250002675) also uses different observation windows and initial temperatures; these require a separately versioned reproduction before comparisons can be combined.

An experimental change routing all wall convection through the room narrowed the CFAST gap but increased some flight sensor residuals. It was **not retained**. The existing wall closures remain assumed. CFAST agreement is not microgravity flight validation.

### Calibration and reproducibility

The numerical model retains the thin-fuel spread/burnout closure, thermally thick growth law, atom-balanced chemistry, finite agent doses and coupled gas transport. Only the documented corrections above changed this revision. Coefficients are regenerated by `calibrate()` in `src/lib/fire/benchmarks.ts`:

| Coefficient | Re-fitted value | Reference / objective |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SIBAL spread | 4.684228 mm/s | 22% O₂, 101.325 kPa, 0.2 m/s; squared log burn-duration errors, IV-1 and VI-2 |
| PMMA average burning flux | 0.821020 g/m²s | 21% O₂, 101.325 kPa, 0.2 m/s |
| PMMA growth | 0.557193 mm/s | Same reference; fitted jointly with flux |
| SIBAL CO/CO₂ | 0.04645535 mol/mol | Squared log cabin CO/CO₂ ratio errors |
| PMMA CO/CO₂ | 0.03769931 mol/mol | Squared log cabin CO/CO₂ ratio errors |

PMMA minimizes squared log errors of peak HRR (weight 1), heat (½), and CO₂ (½), with the same weights as before. Growth is searched in log space; flux and CO ratios use iterative rescaling. Each held-out Saffire prediction refits every coefficient of that material on the other test. No row or failed metric was removed to improve the statistics. IV-1 heat and mean HRR remain excluded from aggregate heat statistics because they are fixed by sample mass times a heat of combustion taken from that same table. Gas statistics still include all four rows.

`IGNIS_EVALUATE=1` runs `src/lib/fire/__probe/evaluate.test.ts`; `IGNIS_EVALUATE_OUT` selects its JSON output. The generated snapshot in `src/lib/fire/accuracy.ts` is checked against a fresh evaluation by `benchmarks.test.ts`, including recalibration from distant starting values. Detailed before/after records are in `.qa-baseline/model-accuracy-20261115/` locally.

The current spread law has no confinement or strip-edge-loss correction; similar inlet atmosphere does not make those experiments geometrically equivalent. The PMMA damage lengths are approximate and are treated as involved length; II-8's structured surface differs from II-9's flat slab.

### Data consistency and remaining limits

The carbon-balance heat estimates remain approximately 428 / 430 / 1655 / 1595 kJ for IV-1 / VI-2 / VI-3 / VI-4, versus published 409 / 580 / 1280 / 830 kJ. VI-2 also publishes 409 kJ from fuel consumption; 580 kJ exceeds its published peak times duration. The 27.6% data-disagreement band is a diagnostic, **not a statistical confidence interval or a reason to declare a failed prediction validated**. A carbon-conserving model cannot exactly match mutually inconsistent heat and gas summaries.

NIST CFAST is rerun on the exported IGNIS heat histories; the current results are in `src/data/fire/cfast-reference.json`. All 13 reference cases must complete and release at least 99% of prescribed heat. It checks cabin response to supplied heat, not predicted burning. Its wall convection assumes 1 g, and the wall/geometry assumptions remain material limitations at 0 g. See `scripts/cfast/README.md` for exact reproduction and assumptions.

Further model development needs synchronized HRR, oxygen, CO₂, CO, temperatures and fan commands, especially VI-4, with measurement uncertainty. Confinement, strip width and oxygen effects need experiments that vary those factors independently before adding fitted response parameters. All lunar/Martian predictions remain extrapolated. Smoke/detector transport, suppression time and temperature fields are reduced-order approximations, not resolved CFD or certified safety predictions.

### Verification

The new protocol tests check off-grid fan-command timing against the analytic fan response, emergency precedence, invalid schedules, identical benchmark/export horizons, and outlet enthalpy conservation. Existing chemistry, transport, thermal, suppression, calibration and UI checks remain enabled. Run `npx vitest run`, `npx tsc --noEmit`, `npm run build`, the Python CFAST tests and the production Playwright suite to reproduce the checks. Final machine-readable results for this revision are stored locally under `.qa-baseline/model-accuracy-20261115/`.

# Earlier record: model v2

Updated 2026-11-14. Implementation follows [SCIENCE-UX-PLAN.md](SCIENCE-UX-PLAN.md). Model version: **2.0.0**.

## What changed

The gas network now exchanges energy with species. Nominal PMMA, cellulose and polyethylene reaction budgets conserve carbon, hydrogen and oxygen. Water vapor, transported unburned fuel vapor, condensed pre-fire aerosols, deposition and remaining fuel have separate inventories. Local oxygen delivery constrains combustion. Chemical HRR is separate from ignition-source heating.

Fuel heating uses implicit one-dimensional conduction for thick solids and a lumped layer for thin/edge cases. Radiation, convection, source shutoff and wall heating retain temperature history. Electrical overload power uses I²R for the assumed circuit. Arc and battery inputs remain prescribed illustrative sources, with finite duration/energy.

Finite CO₂ and chemical-agent doses enter the gas inventories. Water discharge is split into deposited and evaporated mass; captured evaporation is bounded by accessible surface heat and pressure-dependent boiling temperature. Direct calibrated flame/spray transfer is not resolved. Fixed one-second mist extinction and an unlimited local vapor reservoir are no longer imposed.

Flames use a ray-marched shader envelope driven by modeled extent, flow and soot tendency. The default scale is physical; enlargement is an explicit option. Smoke uses solver transport-step history and particle diffusivity; tracer opacity uses an assumed effective mass-extinction coefficient. These are illustrative transport visuals, not a calibrated optical instrument or resolved HVAC CFD. Individual modeled detector locations have their own signals and alarm states.

The navigation has four primary destinations: Home, Fire simulation, NASA research and Results. Set up, Run, Respond and Review share the forecast editor and scenario state. Ask IGNIS is available globally and can explain a captured scenario with real citations or compute a copied fan-shutdown comparison. It is a structured local scenario assistant; a live generative provider is not connected.

## Numerical audit

Run `node scripts/audit-fire-model.cjs` to regenerate `.qa-baseline/v2-physics-audit.json`. It reads the current source without fitting coefficients or modifying data.

- The isolated two-zone energy-exchange example has a **0 J residual**. Tests also check larger transport steps and unequal temperatures.
- PMMA reaction products now contain the nominal fuel carbon inventory. CO₂ is reduced after CO/soot partitioning; the earlier 0.657 kg carbon from 0.600 kg fuel carbon is removed.
- Global oxygen loss matches reaction consumption. Total pyrolysed fuel is accounted for by burned material, transported vapor and retained/removed condensed aerosols.
- For the 600 s lunar PMMA audit case, 0.25 s and 0.125 s public steps both give 528.640 kJ because the internal step is bounded at 0.125 s. A 0.0625 s step gives 528.390 kJ: **about 0.047% difference**. Ignition is 8.5 s in both; alarm time differs by 0.0625 s.
- Finite dose tests check discharge boundaries and repeated actions. Mist tests verify evaporated + deposited water equals released water and the accessible fuel surface does not overcool below incoming water temperature in the tested case.
- Numerical checks do not establish accuracy of spread, yields, detector response, whole-cabin temperature or suppression time in flight.

Measured throughput for the 600 s case is recorded in the JSON report; timing varies with JIT, hardware and simultaneous browser work. It is not a browser/GPU performance guarantee. Browser QA uses installed Edge with software WebGL; native mobile-device frame rates have not been established.

`node scripts/profile-fire-scene.cjs` measured **0.53 fps** over a 5.61 s / three-frame sample at 1600×1000 in forced SwiftShader software rendering during 10× playback. This software-rendered configuration does **not** meet the proposed frame-rate target. The report is `.qa-baseline/v2-render-performance.json`; the captured scene is `.qa-baseline/v2-profiled-scene.png`. No native GPU/mobile performance claim is made, and original render geometry/presets were retained. Test on the intended GPU before a live demonstration; this short sample cannot establish general-device performance.

## Scope of scientific support

All 12 prepared material profiles are retained. Flaming reaction budgets are enabled for PMMA, SIBAL, paper and LDPE. The other eight profiles support exploratory heating/pyrolysis/aerosol behavior while their flaming chemistry is withheld. Complex fluorinated, silicon- and nitrogen-containing product chemistry, acid gases, deflagration and battery cell kinetics are not solved.

Handbook-attributed values without exact verified tables stay labeled as assumed. Parameter exports include units, evidence class, source context, reference pressure, model version and uncertainty notes. The original source measurements and prepared material values are not rewritten to force agreement with model output. Some active coefficients remain empirical or exploratory, including spread, feedback efficiency, effective gasification heat, pre-fire aerosol generation, representative vapor molecular mass, wall properties, exchange geometry, detector response and agent capture.

Buoyant correlations carry gravity and mixture conditions, but their partial-gravity application remains an extrapolation. A nominal lunar or Martian case is not proof that the tested spacecraft geometry behaves the same way. BASS rod/film results, Saffire slab/fabric results and partial-gravity wire results describe distinct configurations. No universal “fastest on the Moon,” blue-at-zero-g, or fan-off extinction rule is claimed.

The three-volume network and detector delay/sensitivity relations approximate spatial transport; they do not solve a three-dimensional velocity field or establish a real detector's response time. Emissivity, opacity and visual flame color are not spectroscopic measurements. Solid-front/spread approximations still need independent experimental evaluation. Gas-network conservation tests do not establish full reacting-flow accuracy.
